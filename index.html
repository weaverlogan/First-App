How the estimate is made, and how to use real data

The model

This is a Bayesian nonparametric model: a Dirichlet process mixture used as a profile regression. Each household is assumed to belong to a hidden group. Every group has its own typical finances and its own chance of financial strain.

The number of groups is not fixed in advance. A Dirichlet process (concentration 1) lets the data decide, and always leaves room for a brand-new group when someone matches nobody well. The model is fitted by collapsed Gibbs sampling, with Normal–Gamma priors on the finances and a Beta prior on each group's strain rate.

Your estimate averages over 60 posterior samples of the grouping. The range therefore reflects two kinds of doubt: which group you belong to, and how risky each group really is. Profiles far from every group get wider ranges automatically.

What financial strain means

In the demo data it means being unable to absorb an unexpected cost of about one month of essential spending, or falling behind on bills, at some point within twelve months. If you load your own file, the outcome is whatever your file records.

Use your own reference data

The demo households are simulated, so results illustrate the method only. To make them meaningful, load a CSV of real households with a header row and these columns:

income, expenses, savings, debt, debt_payment, dependents, variability, insecure

Income, expenses and debt_payment are monthly dollars. Savings and debt are total dollars. Variability runs from 0 (very steady) to 1 (irregular). Insecure is 1 if the household experienced strain and 0 if not. Use plain numbers without commas or symbols. Files with more than 2,000 rows are randomly thinned to keep the page responsive. The file is read on this device only.
